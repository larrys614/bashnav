Your longitude

Needed for local solar time, so the daily atmospheric tide can be taken off the pressure trend before anything is said about it. Without it, an ordinary tropical afternoon reads as a system approaching.

This reasons only from what you logged. It is not a forecast: no model, no chart, no GRIB. It is you, reading your own barometer.

Yours first

      
Nothing of mine until yours is written down. If the app prints its guess
        before asking for yours, you have not forecast anything — you have
        read an answer and agreed with it. It feels like learning and is not.

Now mine

      

      

      
Log the observation this was made for when it comes, then
        look at the score. Three are scored, not one: scoring only you would make
        this an oracle, and it is not one.

    

  


  
  

    

      
The score

      
Weighted error points, from the WxChallenge: half a point
        per knot, one per millibar, a tenth per degree of wind direction —
        so a near miss scores like a near miss. Lower is better.

      

    

    

      
Why persistence is on the sheet

      
“In twelve hours it will be much as it is now” is the honest
        floor. It is hard to beat at short range, and a forecast that does not
        beat it has shown no skill at all. Standard practice in operational
        meteorology, and free to compute.

      
Scoring the rules as well as you keeps the app honest — its misses
        go on the record — and teaches calibration: a rule right seven times
        in ten is useful once you know it is seven in ten. And it lets you
        eventually win, which is the point. A training tool that cannot be
        outgrown is badly built.

From a 500 millibar chart

      
This app cannot fetch a chart and never will. But a 500 mb chart
        arrives by HF radiofax, on an SSB receiver, at sea, with
        no connectivity at all — which is exactly the situation the rest of
        this is built for. Read three numbers off the fax and the rules do the
        rest, showing every step.

The honest limit

      
With no network this cannot forecast. No GRIBs, no
        synoptic charts, no models. What it does is make you your own forecaster
        from the barometer, the sky and the wind.

      
It works entirely in the observation column of the three
        kinds of weather data — ground truth you measured. It cannot
        forecast, but what it reasons from is the one category that is
        never wrong, and the one still available when the antenna comes down.

    

    

      
One shared log

      
This reads the observations deck-log keeps, on
        this device, in the same browser storage — one shared store, as it
        should be. The watch stander says the report once.

      

    

    

      
Never tell before you ask

      
The forecast screen shows nothing of the app’s reasoning until your
        own call is committed to the log. Not greyed out, not collapsed, not
        behind a tap — absent. House rule, and it came from the
        colregs lights quiz telling you the aspect and
        then asking for it.

    

    

      
Where the rules come from

      
The 500 mb rules are from Sienkiewicz and Chesneau,
        Mariner’s Guide to the 500-Millibar Chart, NOAA —
        public domain. The scoring is the WxChallenge’s weighted error
        points. The observation follows the VOS standard. Sources are in
        docs/SOURCES.md; nothing in the lessons is
        copied from anywhere.

Checking…

    

    
Rules of thumb: good ones, and still rules of
      thumb. What is tested is that the app applies its stated rules
      correctly and reports its own score honestly — not that the forecast
      is right. It will often be wrong; that is the nature of the subject, and it
      is why both scorers exist.

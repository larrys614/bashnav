The navigational stars

SHA and declination are computed for the date, with proper motion and precession applied — not copied from a catalogue.

For a date and a DR

Leave Hs empty and this tells you when the sun crosses and how high to expect it. Fill it in afterwards and it gives you the latitude.

For an instant and a longitude

Longitude is what this needs and latitude is what it gives. No limb and no parallax: a star is a point at an infinite distance.

For an instant and a DR

The star horizon is usable between civil and nautical twilight. Before that the horizon is sharp and the stars are not out; after it, the stars are out and the horizon is gone.

A bearing, against the truth

Leave the bearing empty for the body's true azimuth alone. Give the bearing and you get compass error; give the chart variation as well and the error splits into variation and the deviation of your own compass.

Size of the circle

Photograph a patch of sky well up from the horizon with four or more stars you can name. Tap each one and say which it is. No time or position is needed — the angle between two stars is a property of the sky.

What this is worth, and what it is not

Not a sextant. A calibrated phone camera is worth about two to three arcminutes — the same order as a plastic lifeboat sextant, and several times worse than a metal one in practised hands. Uncorrected lens distortion can cost tens of arcminutes, so a sight from an uncalibrated camera is an estimate and must be treated as one.

A tide cannot be computed from a position. It depends on the
      shape of the coast and the depth and resonance of the basin, so every place
      needs constants somebody measured there. You pick a station, not a
      place — and the nearest one in a straight line can be on the wrong
      side of a headland.

What this is

      
Harmonic tide prediction from constants measured at 8,334 stations. It
        runs entirely in the browser with no network, which is the point: a tide
        table is worth most where there is no signal.

      
The synthesis follows Foreman, as carried by UTide — 70
        constituents with their satellite modulations and shallow-water terms.
        The same engine runs in the POSIX awk version of this tool; the
        browser build is checked against it over hundreds of thousands of
        values, and agrees to the last bit or two.

    

    

      
Reference and subordinate stations

      
A reference station has harmonic constants measured
        there, and its curve is a real synthesis.

      
A subordinate station has no constants of its own. It
        carries time and height corrections to a reference station, which is how
        a paper tide table has always worked. For those, the high and low waters
        are the reference’s turns corrected, and the curve between them is
        drawn as a half cosine. There is no honest way to synthesise a continuous
        curve out of offsets, so this build does not pretend to.

    

    

      
Times, datums and what is missing

      
Times are the station’s own standard time —
        no summer time, exactly like a printed tide table. The datum is named on
        the day’s screen; heights are above it.

      
Nothing here knows about the weather. A deep low or a hard onshore blow
        moves the real water by a half metre or more, and a river in flood moves
        it further. Harmonics predict the astronomical tide and nothing else.
